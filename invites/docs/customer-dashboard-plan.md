# The customer's dashboard: one tab per tool, and a page that answers as you type

Part 1 is for the owner and uses her words only. Parts 2 to 4 are for the engineer; every file and line reference was checked against the code on the day this was written (13 September 2026), against the map of our own customer side drawn the same day, and against a screen recording and twenty-odd screenshots of wooowinvites.com's customer dashboard.

The one idea underneath everything: **the customer's job is to insert their details and watch the page take them; ours is to curate the designs.** wooowinvites sells do-it-yourself, so their editor is full of levers we never want a customer to touch (themes, blocks, envelopes, music libraries). What is worth copying is not the levers. It is the *shape* of the dashboard: edit on the left and see the real page on the right, a checklist that is also the plan, one tab per big tool with the numbers first, cards that explain themselves before they ask for anything, empty states that say what will appear, and everything saving on its own.

Two things the owner asked us to leave out on purpose: **Planning** (checklist and budget tracker) and the **Betting Game**. Neither is in this plan.

## 1. What changes, in her words

### What a customer sees today

After paying, a customer lands on a dashboard with cards stacked down the page (what to have ready, your form, your dates, publish & share, four numbers, latest RSVPs) and a list called "Manage" at the side, one line per tool. Each tool is its own page with only a "← back" link to the dashboard. The form to fill in their details is behind "Edit invitation" (or, for Done-For-You, "Your details & preview" — a different form for the same details, with every part stacked in one long accordion). In the builder the preview on the right only changes after they press one of three save buttons, and every time it changes it starts again from the sealed envelope, which they have to tap open and scroll down. On a phone the preview sits under the whole form.

### What a customer will see

**A strip of tabs across the top of every page about their invitation.** Invitation · Share · Guest list · RSVP responses · Seating chart · Check-in · Messages · Guestbook · Guest photos · Save the Date · Link & design · Guide. Tools their package does not include stay in the strip with an *Upgrade* pill, so they can see what exists. A Save the Date card shows only the tabs a Save the Date has (Invitation, Share, Link & design, Guide, and a tab back to the invitation it announces). Above the strip, on every tab: the invitation's name, whether it is a draft or live, and two buttons — **Preview** and **Publish** (which reads **Share** once it is live).

**The Invitation tab is the form, in two panes.** On the left, what they fill in; on the right, a phone the size of a real phone showing their actual page, which stays put while the left scrolls. Every change saves by itself — there is no save button to forget — and the phone shows the change a moment later, at the part they are editing, without playing the envelope again. A line under the form says "Saved" or "Saving…" so they never wonder. On a phone, a *Preview* button floats at the bottom and opens the same phone view over the form.

**A Get-started checklist at the top of the form.** Six lines with ticks and a progress bar: *Your cover*, *Your dates*, *Your photos*, *Your words*, *Your RSVP*, *Publish and share*. Each tick is earned by the page itself, not by pressing "Done": the cover is ticked when the names, date and venue are in; the photos when a cover photograph is up; and so on. Under it, **Show me around** walks through the screen in eight steps (the tabs, the steps, the form, the saving, the phone, Done, Publish, the Guide) and **Don't show again** puts the checklist away.

**Steps in the order of the page.** The parts to fill in are listed as numbered steps in the order the design shows them — Cover, Our Story, The Invitation, Entourage, Photos, Venue, Dress code… — so as they work down the list, the phone scrolls down the page with them. The old "Done — save & fold away" button becomes a tick on each step: *Mark this part done*. Marking every part done still tells our team the form is complete, exactly as before.

**One form for everyone.** A Done-For-You customer fills in the same Invitation tab; the separate intake accordion goes. The three ways of sending details stay: fill the form, or send it over Messenger, or send an Excel — the second and third are a card in the checklist ("Prefer to send it to us?") that tells our team to expect it. Their preview-and-approval thread (open the preview, approve, request changes) moves to the Share tab, where publishing happens.

**The Share tab** is where the link lives: the Publish button with its named blanks and the tick, then once live the link, a QR code they can download on its own, the Messenger/Viber/WhatsApp/SMS buttons, the card image, the print view, and the Save the Date pair. For Done-For-You, the approval thread sits at the top of this tab until it is published.

**The Guide tab** has a chapter per tab, in plain words, with Previous and Next, and a list of common questions. It follows the same order as the tabs.

### Later phases (in order)

- **Seating chart as its own tab**, with Tables · Seated · Empty seats at the top, table cards you drag names onto, an *Unassigned* list of chips, a search box, and the seat-finder on the guest's own link (which we already have).
- **Guest list reorganised**: numbers first, then *Add a guest* and *Import a list* as cards that say what they need ("a name, and a phone or e-mail so the personal link finds them"), then the list, then reminders. A warning when an import would make duplicates.
- **RSVP responses with the numbers drawn**: attendance as bars, meals and dietary needs as counts, the guests' messages as cards, and a *Preview the RSVP form* button that opens the phone at the form.
- **History**: every auto-save kept for a while, with *Restore*.
- **Suggested writings on every writing box**, in the occasion's own words (a christening's, a debut's), as tap-to-use examples — we already do this on the hardest boxes.
- **Guestbook and Guest photos switched on from their own tabs**, not from a toggle in the form.

### What the screenshots showed, and where each thing lives with us

Everything else on their screens — blocks, the envelope generator, background themes, block fonts and colours, dividers, borders — is them handing the design to the customer. That is the do-it-yourself we do not sell.

**What we lay out for the customer to give us.** The form already asks, part by part: the names and the cover photograph · dates, times, ceremony and reception (the maps come from the address) · parents and entourage · the story and its photos · dress code and motif colours · the gift note and GCash or bank QR · FAQ · the song · the RSVP deadline and questions · the closing message · spare photos and files at the end, kept for us to pull from. The Get-started list ticks itself from what is filled, so they can see what is still owed. For Done-For-You the "Prefer to send it to us?" card stays (Messenger, Viber, Excel), and marking every part done moves the job to *intake received* for the team.

**What they see in the account, and how it is explained.**

- *Live (phase 1, #156):* the tab strip on every page; the two-pane Invitation tab with auto-save and a phone that jumps to the part being edited; Opening on/off on the phone; the tour; the Share tab (publish, link, QR download, share buttons, the Done-For-You approval thread); the Guide with a chapter per tab.
- *Phase 2 (#157):* **Seating chart** as its own tab (Tables · Seated · Empty seats; drag names onto tables; numbered seats; a table's shape). **Guest list** in working order (numbers → *Add a guest* and *Import your list* as cards that explain themselves → the list → reminders; duplicates skipped on import and said so). **RSVP responses** with the numbers drawn (Responses · Attending · Not attending · Average party; attendance bars; meal and dietary counts; messages as cards) and a *What guests see* phone showing the real RSVP form with the questions as switches beside it — turn one on and it is on the form at once — plus the sentence about the automatic thank-you and the confirmation e-mail. **Guestbook** and **Guest photos** switched on and off, and moderated, from their own tabs.
- *Phase 3 (#159):* History with Restore; a starting point in every writing box.
- *The welcome:* after checkout the customer lands on the Invitation tab, not on a receipt with a button. Over the Get-started list, the welcome carries the receipt — the order, the payment confirmed — and lays out the plan, and offers the tour once: *Show me around* or *I'll find my way*. The answer is remembered on the invitation (`welcomedAt`), so a phone and a laptop agree, and a first open on a closed browser is offered again. The activation e-mail and notice already open the tab, so a manual payer verified days later meets the same welcome.

**Packages, and how it syncs.** One table in the code (`FEATURE_MIN_TIER` in `src/lib/tiers.ts`) decides what each package gets, and everything reads it: the comparison on the landing page, checkout, the upgrade page, the padlocks in the form, the tabs (locked → Upgrade) and the guest's page. **Next.** Phases 1 to 3 and the welcome are live. What remains from their screens is the studio's: the levers — dividers, side borders, opening and envelope styles, block-level backgrounds — go onto the studio backlog, ours to build and priced into the template.

### What she does not need to change

Nothing about the designs, the studio, the guest page or the packages changes. The customer's form still asks exactly what the design asks; the fixed writings are still ours; the three-week change window and the revision rounds still hold.

## 2. Why this shape (what the two maps said)

Our map (drawn by six readers over the customer side on 13 Sep 2026) and the wooow map agree on what makes a dashboard read as systematic. wooow does seven things we do not:

1. **Edit left, see right, always** — their phone preview never leaves the screen where you build. Ours reloads only after a save, replays the opening every time, and on a phone is under the form (`components/builder/builder.tsx:101, 227-245`).
2. **One tab per big tool, with counters first** — Pending · Attending · Declined; Tables · Seated · Empty. Ours are separate pages reachable only through the dashboard's "Manage" list, with no strip between them (`account/invitations/[id]/page.tsx:226-248`).
3. **A checklist that is the plan**, ticked by the product, with a tour that walks the same steps and a guide in the same order. Ours counts self-reported "Done" presses (`lib/progress.ts`), and 100% can be reached with nothing filled.
4. **Auto-save**, with "Auto-save on" in the header. Ours has three save buttons and loses typing silently when a section pill is clicked (`builder/page.tsx:76`, `builder.tsx:62`).
5. **Every card explains itself first** and states what it needs in words. Ours is good on the form's hints and weak on the tools (the Guest list opens on reminder blasts, not the list).
6. **Empty states say what will appear here.**
7. **A Guide in the customer's words**, chapter per tab.

What we have that wooow does not, and keep: the personal-link RSVP with seat caps and companions, the seats-to-settle queue, the printable headcount sheet, four ways to import guests, reminders that show the cost before sending, check-in with a party-size stepper, the three-week window and the revision rounds, Done-For-You. The plan touches none of their logic; it moves them into tabs and puts their numbers first.

What we deliberately do not copy: themes, blocks, envelopes, music library, languages as separate links, categories and passwords per block, custom URL requests, Planning, Betting Game, Memories as a separate product (our guest photos stay a tab).

## 3. Phase 1 — the frame, the form, the preview, the share, the guide

### 3.1 The strip and the header — `account/invitations/[id]/layout.tsx`

A nested layout under `/account/invitations/[id]` renders, for every child page: `← My invitations`, the title, `InvitationPill`, occasion · package · design, the **Preview** link (`/{slug}?preview=1`, new tab) and the **Publish/Share** button (`/account/invitations/[id]/share`), then the tab strip. The layout loads the invitation with `ownInvitation` (`lib/guard.ts:74`); a child page loads it again — one extra `findUnique` per request, which is what the studio already does for its own header.

The tabs are a pure function in `lib/account-tabs.ts`: `tabsFor({ id, saveTheDate, pairId, tier, addOns, dfy, published })` → `{ key, label, href, locked?, show }[]`, so the strip and the tests read one list. Locked tabs keep their label and carry an *Upgrade* pill linking to `/upgrade`, exactly as the Manage list did (`page.tsx:243-246`). The tab is marked current by the pathname prefix (`components/account/tabs.tsx`, a client component with `usePathname`). On narrow screens the strip scrolls sideways; the current tab is scrolled into view on mount.

Every tab page drops its own `← {title}` link and keeps its `PageHeader` as the tab's own heading (guests, rsvps, checkin, messages, guestbook, photos, settings, upgrade, dfy).

### 3.2 The Invitation tab — `account/invitations/[id]/page.tsx`

The page that was the dashboard becomes the builder. `builder/page.tsx` redirects to `..?section=` so every existing link still lands. The dashboard's cards go where they belong: *Publish & share* → the Share tab; the four numbers and *Latest RSVPs* → the RSVP responses tab (which already has them); *Your dates* and *What to have ready* → lines in the Get-started checklist; *Extra photos* → the Extras step; the Save the Date pair → its own tab.

**Order of steps.** `sectionOrder(occasion, template.layout)` (`lib/sections.ts:1120`) instead of `sectionsFor` alone, so the steps follow the design's page order. Sections the design does not carry keep their place at the end, as `sectionOrder` already does.

**Two panes.** `lg:grid-cols-[minmax(0,1fr)_27rem]`. Left: the checklist card, the numbered step list (a wrapping row of pills; the current one filled), the step's heading and description, the fields, and a sticky footer with the Done tick and the save state. Right: the phone, `lg:sticky lg:top-4`. The section nav's third column goes; the form gets the room it was missing at 1024px.

**Auto-save.** `Builder` keeps `value` as now; a 700 ms debounce after the last change calls `saveSectionAction` (`account/actions.ts:35`); saves are queued so two never overlap and the latest value always lands last. The footer reads *Saving…* / *Saved* (with the time) / *Saved, with notes: …*, and the notes are worded from the field's label rather than its path (`fields.find(f => f.key === path)?.label`). Leaving the step with a save in flight is fine — the save is a server action holding the value it was given. A `beforeunload` guard covers the half-second between the last keystroke and the save. Photos already upload on choose (`fields.tsx:303-313`).

**Done is a tick.** *Mark this part done* calls `sectionDoneAction` (`actions.ts:46`); the counter and `completedAt` logic in `saveSection` (`invitations.ts:265-274`) are untouched. The three save buttons go. *Next: {label}* is one link, always to the next step in order.

**The phone.** `.phone` grows to a real handset: 390 CSS px of page inside a 10 px bezel (`globals.css:247`). The iframe loads `/{slug}?bare=1&at={section}`. `bare` is honoured for a previewer already (`[slug]/shared.tsx:137`) and drops the opening, the music and the day/night toggle. `at` is new: `[slug]/page.tsx` passes it through to `InvitationPage`, which renders a small client `<ScrollTo id>` that scrolls the section's anchor into view once the page has laid out (the anchors exist: `renderer.tsx:110, 405, 584, 904…`; the cover is `#top`). Two iframes are kept and swapped on `load`, so the page never blanks between saves. The "Preview — not published yet" banner is not drawn inside a bare view: it is the builder's own frame, and the banner ate the top of the cover at phone width (`renderer.tsx:2108`).

On a phone (below `lg`) the right pane is hidden and a floating **Preview** button opens the same phone in a full-screen sheet.

**The checklist.** `lib/checklist.ts` — `checklistFor({ occasion, content, tier, addOns, status, saveTheDate, layout })` → `{ key, label, done, href, hint? }[]`, pure and tested:

| Line | Done when | Opens |
|---|---|---|
| Your cover | `publishProblems` reports nothing for the cover (`sections.ts:1514`) | `?section=cover` |
| Your dates | `cover.sendOut` is set (`sections.ts:271`) | `?section=cover` |
| Your photos | a cover photo is present (`coverImage(content)`, `sections.ts:1643`) | `?section=cover` |
| Your words | every unlocked section that does not hide when empty is filled, or is marked Done | the first one that is not |
| Your RSVP | `rsvp.deadline` is set (`sections.ts:663`) | `?section=rsvp` |
| Publish and share | `status === 'PUBLISHED'` | `/share` |

A Save the Date card has three lines (cover, dates, publish). The tour is `components/account/tour.tsx`: an overlay that reads `data-tour="tabs|steps|form|saving|phone|done|publish|guide"` off the page, positions a card beside the target, and steps with Next / Back / Finish; *Don't show again* is a `localStorage` key per invitation.

**Done-For-You.** `IntakeForm` (`dfy/forms.tsx:15-107`) is retired: the builder is the form for every customer, so there is one place their details live and the phone shows what they typed. When every section is marked Done and a `DfyJob` sits at `NEW`, `saveSection` moves it to `INTAKE_RECEIVED` (the same move `saveIntake` made on submit, `lib/dfy.ts:50`) and staff are notified as now. The Messenger and Excel choices become one card in the checklist, *Prefer to send it to us?*, whose button calls `saveIntakeAction` with the chosen method and `submit: true` — the existing action, with no content. `saveIntake` stays for staff and for the encoder workspace.

### 3.3 The Share tab — `account/invitations/[id]/share/page.tsx`

Top to bottom: for Done-For-You, the stage pills, the stage notice and the `RevisionThread` (from `dfy/page.tsx:69-101`, unchanged); then `PublishControls` (`controls.tsx:48-75`) with its problems, blanks and agreement tick; once live, the link, `ShareBox`, a **Download QR** link (`/{slug}/qr.svg`, a route that returns `qrSvg(url, { size: 1024 })`), and the Save the Date pair card. `dfy/page.tsx` redirects here.

### 3.4 The Guide tab — `account/invitations/[id]/guide/page.tsx`

A static page: `lib/guide-chapters.ts` holds the chapters as data (`{ key, title, body: string[], tab }`), one per tab in the strip's order, plus *Common questions*. Chapters whose tab is locked for this package still show, with the *Upgrade* line. Previous / Next at the foot of each chapter.

### 3.5 Other pages that move

- `account/page.tsx:64-66`: the card's buttons become **Open** (the Invitation tab) and **View live**; *Send details* / *Build status* / *Edit* go.
- `checkout/confirm/[reference]/page.tsx:45-46`: *Open the intake form* → **Fill in your details**, to the Invitation tab.
- `lib/orders.ts:221-231` and `settings-defaults.ts:93-94`: the activation e-mail and notice link to the Invitation tab.
- `account/notifications`: unchanged (its links open the tab they name).

### 3.6 Tests

- `tests/account-tabs.test.ts`: the strip for a wedding on each tier; a Save the Date's strip; locked tabs are present with `locked`; the Save the Date tab shows only with a pair or the entitlement.
- `tests/checklist.test.ts`: each line's rule; a fresh invitation ticks nothing; a published one ticks the last; a Save the Date has three lines.
- `tests/progress.test.ts`, `tests/dfy` (if present): the job moves to `INTAKE_RECEIVED` when the form completes.
- `tests/client/builder.test.ts`: the debounce coalesces three edits into one save and the last value wins.

## 4. Phases 2 and 3

**Phase 2 — the tools.** Seating chart tab (`/seating`: `SeatingTable.shape` column, table cards with numbered seats and drag-and-drop from an *Unassigned* list, search, counters, delete with confirmation; the two cards leave the guest manager, `guests/manager.tsx:150-236`). Guest list reorganised (`guests/page.tsx`: counters → Add a guest card → Import card → the list → reminders; duplicate check on import, `lib/guests.ts:88-135`). RSVP responses (`rsvps/page.tsx`: Responses · Attending · Not attending · Average party; attendance bars; meal and dietary counts; messages as cards; *Preview the RSVP form* → `/{slug}?bare=1&at=rsvp`). Guestbook and Guest photos tabs get their on/off switch (writes `guestbook.enabled` / `photos.enabled` through `saveSectionAction`).

**Phase 3 — the words and the safety net.** `InvitationRevision` (id, invitationId, content, title, createdAt): one row per auto-save that changed something, the last 30 kept plus one per day for 60 days; a **History** button in the header with *Restore*, which first saves the current state as a revision. Suggested writings on every writing field, from `wordsFor(occasion)` and the look's lines (`lib/design.ts`, `lib/looks.ts`), as the tap-to-use chips the message fields already have (`fields.tsx:100-128`).
